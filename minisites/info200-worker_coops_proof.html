Worker cooperatives are no exception. Much of what is produced is made freely available online in a wide variety of formats ### Online resource directories Many organizations in the cooperative ecosystem make information accesisble to the public on their websites. Resources range from statistical information and reports about the cooperative economy, to member maps, to guides to cooperative history. Examples: * <a target="_blank" href="https://nobawc.org/map-of-nobawc-coops/">NoBAWC's member map</a>, a map of Bay Area worker cooperatives * <a target="_blank" href="https://www.usworker.coop/shopcoop/">USFWC's member-submitted list of cooperative-made products</a> * <a target="_blank" href="https://uwcc.wisc.edu/resources/worker-cooperatives/">University of Wisconsin's list of resources for worker cooperatives,</a> including both relevant reports from various providers and examples of worker cooperatives in Wisconsin * <a target="_blank" href="https://neweconomy.net/black-coop-history/">New Economy Coalition's Black Co-op History</a> resource list ### Online courses A few cooperative organizations offer self-paced online classes relating to topics specific to worker cooperatives. Many are free, and are targeted from people new to cooperatives to long-running businesses. Examples: * <a target="_blank" href="https://startcoop.teachable.com/p/lean-co-op">Start Coop's introduction to starting a lean co-op</a> * <a href="https://academy.lacooplab.com/home?preview=true" target="_blank">L.A. Coop Lab</a> offers variety of courses from a few different organizations. Topics include starting a coop, an introduction to worker cooperatives in Spanish and English, and more * <a href="https://ncbaclusa.coop/learning-hub/" target="_blank">National Cooperative Business Association's customizable education tools</a> for the wider cooperative community (i.e. not just worker cooperatives) ### Blogs and user-generated resources Many worker cooperatives and regional cooperative groups have active news sections on their websites, including links to aggregated resources like member maps, lectures, events, and oral histories. Additionally, the larger cooperative community has created many ways to collate information and often offer them as standalone websites. Additionally, the US worker cooperative community has many longform videos available on YouTube, ranging from how-tos, to topics relating to social justice and the wider solidarity economy, to simply sharing cooperative stories. Because cooperation among cooperatives and education of members are core cooperative principles ([[ICA, 1995->References]]), the cooperative community is particularly active in creating accessible educational resources with personal connection at the core. ### Email lists The local cooperative network has <a target="_blank" href="https://nobawc.org/newsletter-listservs/">active email lists</a> for announcements, where you can learn about upcoming events thrown by local cooperatives, and a more active discussion list only for network members. ### Peer networks and mentorship circles The U.S Federation of Worker Cooperatives coordinates <a target="_blank" href="https://www.usworker.coop/programs/peer-networks/">peer networks</a>, all of which are accessible online. Peer networks connect members of cooperatives around different themes, including member identity, business sector, and role. Some mentorship circles meet remotely over video chat and/or communicate over chat software regularly, while others exist only as active email lists.
